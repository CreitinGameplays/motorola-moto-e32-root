3. Why no progress bar was visible - geometry, settled with vendor data
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
The logo partition (sda12) is a container of gzip-compressed BMPs; all
boot/charge images are 720x1600, 24 bpp, and their *content* is drawn
rotated 90 degrees inside the BMP (e.g. the battery-charging image is
stored lying on its side).  The bootloader therefore transposes the
picture on the way into lcd_base:

  memory buffer = 1600x720 LANDSCAPE raster, stride 4800 B, 720 rows
  panel         = 720x1600 portrait, displaying the raster ROTATED:
                  physical X = buffer row, physical Y = buffer column

This matches the cmdline (lcd_size=1600x720, logo_bpix=24) and explains
the v1 photo exactly: v1 painted text at buffer rows 14..48 / columns
341..1259, which shows up as a narrow vertical strip near the left edge,
starting ~21% down the panel and ending ~79% - precisely what the photo
shows.  The stride that v1 used (4800) was correct; the picture was
simply rotated.

Consequences:
 * v2's change to a 2160-byte stride was wrong - it sheared every glyph
   row (the "still rendering with visual artifacts" report of v2).
 * v3's progress bar painted buffer rows 0..1 across columns 8..1592.
   Under the transposition that is physical X = 0..1 (a 2-px sliver on
   the extreme left edge) and physical Y = 8..1592 (its whole length).
   Two dim gray pixels at the very edge of a blue logo are invisible,
   which is why "no bar appeared" - nothing failed, the bar was simply
   in the wrong place.

Correct mapping for the new indicator: to place something on the panel,
paint buffer row = physical X and buffer column = physical Y.  A bar
along the physical top edge is an 8-px-wide strip at buffer columns
0..7 across all 720 rows; the mirrored strip at columns 1592..1599 is
the physical bottom edge.
